**Observations:**

1. Both runs behave alike, although they differ in integrator, start values, configuration and number of bodies.
   The selection changes the direction by at most about 15° (largest late on, S1 against S3).
2. **The mean direction passes 180° and swings beyond it.**
   - It passes 180° at 320–330 Myr (G201) and 350–360 Myr (M2), in agreement with the first long WHFast run
     (`docs/cluster-direction.md`: 340–350 Myr).
   - It reaches its smallest value at about 500–560 Myr: 127–135° (G201), 140–146° (M2).
   - After that it turns back towards 180°: G201 is at 151–161° by 820 Myr.
   - This looks like a swing about 180° that has not settled yet. 820 Myr do not show whether it settles, or with
     what amplitude.
3. **R falls while the direction swings.** R peaks at 0.64 (S2, 140–150 Myr) and drops to 0.25 (M2, 640 Myr) and
   0.15 (G201, 820 Myr). The direction is still significant (Rayleigh p < 0.01 with about 800–1,600 bodies in the selection), but
   the cluster gets weaker.
4. **For the main run's criteria (with this note):**
   - A single snapshot before about 1 Gyr says little about anti-alignment: the mean direction swings between about 130° and 265° in the first 800 Myr.
   - R is not monotonic in time.
   - Criteria on Δϖ need either late times (Brown & Batygin compare after 4 Gyr) or an average over a long window. They should be stated for each of the three selections.

## Are the planets of the 8 M2 slices bit-identical?

The concern: during a close encounter MERCURIUS integrates the bodies involved with IAS15, possibly including the
planet. The planets of different slices would then differ in the last bits afterwards and drift apart chaotically.

Check: `tools/planet_hash.py`. It takes the sha256 of the float64 masses, positions and velocities of all massive
bodies (Sun, Jupiter to Neptune, Planet Nine) at every snapshot, from `p9sim dump`, which prints 17 significant
digits.

- **0–640 Myr, all 8 slices, all 640 snapshots (1 per Myr, including every segment end): one hash at every time;
  the largest position difference is 0 for every planet.**
- After 640 Myr, comparing each slice with slice 01 at every segment end it shares (49–53 ends per pair, up to
  690–730 Myr): no difference.
- The reason, in REBOUND 5.2.2 `src/integrator_mercurius.c`, `reb_mercurius_encounter_step`:
  - The massive bodies' states after the Kepler step are kept (`particles_backup`, flag `tponly_encounter`) and
    restored when only massless test particles take part in the encounters.
  - p9sim sets `testparticle_type = 0` (`src/p9sim.c`), so the planets do not feel the test particles.
  - In this run every encounter is a test particle with a planet, never two massive bodies, so the planets keep
    their Kepler-step states bit for bit.
- Measuring Δϖ against the Planet Nine of the same slice is therefore the same as against any slice's Planet Nine.
  `tools/cluster_direction.py` uses the first slice's Planet Nine for all of them.
- This holds only as long as no two massive bodies come close, and only with REBOUND's handling of test-particle-only
  encounters. The check should be repeated for other configurations or REBOUND versions.

Output: `runs/night-20261024/eval/planets-0-200.*`, `planets-0-640.*` and `planets-after-640.txt` (not in git).
