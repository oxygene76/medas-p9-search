# Pilot long run, night of 18/19 October 2026

Test case after Brown & Batygin (2021): Sun (+ inner planets), Jupiter–Neptune
(DE440s, JD 2458270.0), Planet Nine (6.2 Earth masses, a = 380 au, q = 300 au,
i = 16°), 4,000 test particles (a 150–550 au, q 30–50 au, i 0–10°; seed 9).
REBOUND 5.2.2 WHFast, democratic heliocentric, dt = 0.5 yr.

## Run (`runs/long-20261018/`, `tools/campaign.py`, commit 3eaa32e)

- 16 chains of 250 test particles, one CPU core each (16 of 24 cores; nice 10),
  segments of 20 Myr, a snapshot per Myr. 22:27 to 06:47 (deadline 06:55).
- Reached 360–420 Myr per chain (all chains: 360 Myr); 317 segments, 131.8 CPU
  hours, 303 MB.
- Speed: median 48.7 Myr per hour and chain (41.9 at the start, 67.5 after 340
  Myr as particles are removed), i.e. the 4,000 particles together at about
  45–50 Myr per hour on 16 cores; 202 ns per particle and step on average
  (mixed performance and efficiency cores, fully loaded machine; an idle
  performance core needs 110 ns).
- Survivors: 3,946 (20 Myr), 3,643 (80), 3,242 (140), 2,974 (200), 2,777 (260),
  2,656 (320), 2,597 (360).
- Planet Nine is bit-identical in all 16 chains at every snapshot checked.
- Energy error of the massive bodies about 3e-7, bounded.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 4,000 | 1,270 | 0.15, 338° (*) | 0.01, – |

(*) The start values are uniform in heliocentric elements; the conversion to
barycentric elements makes the selected distant particles slightly non-uniform.

At 360 Myr the distant particles are anti-aligned with Planet Nine in the
longitude of perihelion (Δϖ ≈ 180°) and aligned in the node (ΔΩ ≈ 0°), the
configuration of Brown & Batygin; the clustering is still evolving (the paper
compares after 4 Gyr).

## GPU comparison

The same 4,000 particles on the GPU (p9gpu, `docs/gpu-integrator.md`) to 300
Myr agree statistically at every checked time (survivors, distributions of a,
q, i, clustering of Δϖ and ΔΩ; Kolmogorov-Smirnov p ≥ 0.08):
`runs/compare-rebound-p9gpu-*.txt` in the development worktree.

## Projection: the full run over 4 Gyr

Particle-steps: 1.4e13 if the survivors keep falling as fitted (logarithmic in
time: about 2,000 at 1 Gyr, 1,150 at 4 Gyr), 2.1e13 if no further particle is
lost after 360 Myr.

| | 1.4e13 particle-steps | 2.1e13 particle-steps |
|---|---|---|
| CPU (this machine, loaded mix of cores, 202 ns) | about 790 core-hours | about 1,190 core-hours |
| CPU (idle performance cores, 110 ns) | about 430 core-hours | about 650 core-hours |
| Wall time with 16 such cores | 2–3 days | 2.5–3 days |
| p9gpu on the RTX 5080 Laptop GPU (2.1e8 particle-steps/s, measured over 300 Myr) | about 19 h | about 28 h |

With 16 cores at the measured 45–50 Myr per hour, 4 Gyr take about 80–90 hours.
The laptop GPU replaces about 40–60 loaded CPU cores for this problem.
